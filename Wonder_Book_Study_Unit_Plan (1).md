## 3. THE WEEKLY RHYTHM (applies every week, Weeks 1–9)

| Day | Mode | App Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Teacher reads aloud | Slideshow/PowerPoint-style screen: one screen per chunk (cover slide → lead-in questions → read-aloud slide with page range → comprehension/inference questions → final "go-away" reflection prompt). Teacher drives with next/back arrows. |
| **Tuesday** | Group reading (groups of 4, take turns reading) | Same content, but delivered as a **worksheet mode**: toggle between on-screen interactive (type answers into app) and **printable PDF** version. Timer/turn-tracker optional for group reading. |
| **Wednesday** | Teacher reads aloud | Same as Monday format, next reading chunk. |
| **Thursday** | Group reading | Same as Tuesday format, next reading chunk. |
| **Friday** | **Quiz day** | 20-question comprehensive quiz covering that week's four reading chunks. Auto-marked in-app; printable version with answer key for teacher. |

Every lesson (Mon–Thu) = **45 minutes**, structured as:
1. Settle & lead-in questions (5 min)
2. Reading (15–20 min)
3. Comprehension/inference questions — discussion or written (15 min)
4. Go-away reflection prompt, recorded in reading journal (5 min)

Each week also has **one rotating menu of 4 projects** (poster / comic strip / drama-acting scene / journal reflection) tied to that week's events. Groups/individuals can be assigned one, or choose — teacher's call. These are done in spare/extension time across the week, not inside the 45-minute reading lesson.

### WEEK 1 — Meeting Auggie

**Learning Intention:** We can identify how a character's perspective shapes how a story is told, and infer a character's feelings from what they say and don't say.
**Success Criteria:** I can describe August's feelings about starting school and give evidence from the text; I can make a prediction based on clues in the story.

**Monday — Teacher read:** *Ordinary → Christopher's House*
- Lead-in: If a genie granted you one wish, what would it be — and why? Do you think everyone's answer would be different?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why does August say he's "not an ordinary kid" but also feels ordinary inside? What does this tell us about the difference between how people see us and how we see ourselves? Why do you think August avoids describing exactly what he looks like?
- Go-away prompt: Write 3 sentences about something people might misjudge about you before they get to know you.

**Tuesday — Group read:** *Driving → Nice Mrs. Garcia*
- Lead-in: How would you feel meeting a brand-new school and brand-new people for the very first time, aged 10?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why is August's mum being so protective before the school tour? What clues tell us Mr. Tushman is trying to make August feel comfortable?
- Go-away prompt: Describe one adult in your life who makes you feel safe when you're nervous, and how they do it.

**Wednesday — Teacher read:** *Jack Will, Julian, and Charlotte → The Deal*
- Lead-in: If you had to show a new student around your school, what three things would you point out first?
- Comprehension/Inference: What is "the deal" Mr. Tushman makes, and why might that be a smart plan — or an unfair one? Compare Jack, Julian, and Charlotte's first reactions to August — what's different about each?
- Go-away prompt: Predict which of the three kids will become August's real friend. Give one clue from the text to back up your guess.

**Thursday — Group read:** *Home → Locks*
- Lead-in: Think of your own first day at a new place (school, club, team). What worried you most?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why might August feel both excited and terrified on the night before school? What does the "combination lock" scene reveal about how nervous he is?
- Go-away prompt: Write a short diary entry as August, the night before his first day.

**Weekly Project Menu (choose or assign one):**
1. **Poster:** Design a "Welcome to Beecher Prep" poster from Mr. Tushman's office, including at least 3 things a new student should know.
2. **Comic strip:** 6-panel comic showing August's morning routine before his first day of school.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Act out the car ride to school (Driving) in groups of 3 — August, Mum, Dad — showing the mixed emotions.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write a letter from August to his future self, to be read on his last day of Year 5.

**Friday Quiz 1 — 20 Questions (Teacher answer key)**
1. What does August say makes him "ordinary"? — *He does ordinary kid things: eats ice cream, rides his bike, plays video games.*
2. How many surgeries has August had by the story's start? — *27.*
3. Why has August never been to a real school before? — *Because of his surgeries/appearance and his parents' choice to homeschool him.*
4. Who is Christopher? — *August's childhood best friend, who moved away.*
5. What game did August and Christopher love playing together? — *Star Wars, with action figures/lightsabers.*
6. What school is August about to start at? — *Beecher Prep.*
7. Who is the principal/school leader August meets? — *Mr. Tushman.*
8. What joke does August's dad make before the school visit? — *About Mr. Tushman's funny surname over the loudspeaker.*
9. Who works the front desk and everyone calls "Mrs. G"? — *Mrs. Garcia.*
10. Name the three students Mr. Tushman picks to show August around. — *Jack Will, Julian, and Charlotte.*
11. What is "the deal" Mr. Tushman makes with the three students? — *He asks them to be welcoming/show August around and be kind to him.*
12. Which of the three seems most reluctant or awkward about meeting August? — *Julian (accept reasoned answers, e.g. Julian is standoffish).*
13. What does August worry about most on the night before school starts? — *Fitting in, being stared at, whether kids will be nice.*
14. What object does August struggle with the night before school (mentioned in "Locks")? — *His combination lock.*
15. Inference: Why might August's parents have waited until Year 5 (age 10) to send him to school? — *Accept reasoned answers: enough surgeries were done, they wanted to protect him, they felt he was ready.*
16. Inference: What does it suggest about August's character that he still makes jokes with his dad despite being nervous? — *Accept reasoned answers: he has a good sense of humour, resilience, close family bond.*
17. Vocabulary: What does "petrified" mean, as August uses it about starting school? — *Extremely frightened.*
18. True or False: August has an older sister named Via. — *True.*
19. Why do you think the author chose to start the book from August's own point of view? — *Accept reasoned answers: to build empathy, to show his inner ordinary self before others judge his appearance.*
20. Short answer: In your own words, what is "The Deal" really asking the three students to do, and why might that be hard for a Year 5 student? — *Open answer, mark for understanding of peer pressure/social risk.*

### WEEK 2 — Settling In & the Precepts

**Learning Intention:** We can infer how setting and social dynamics (like seating at lunch) reveal character relationships.
**Success Criteria:** I can explain why August finds lunchtime difficult and identify the message behind Mr. Browne's precepts.

**Monday — Teacher read:** *First-Day Jitters → Around the Room*
- Lead-in: Describe a time you walked into a room and felt like everyone was looking at you.
- Comprehension/Inference: What does August notice first when he walks into the classroom? Why do you think the author includes so much detail about people's reactions/stares?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a rule you'd want a whole school to follow, and why.

**Tuesday — Group read:** *Lamb to the Slaughter → Choose Kind*
- Lead-in: What do you think the saying "choose kind" means in your own words?
- Comprehension/Inference: What is Mr. Browne's "precept," and why does he ask the class to write their own each month? Why might "Lamb to the Slaughter" be a scary chapter title for August's first day?
- Go-away prompt: Write your own class precept for this month — a short saying about how to treat others.

**Wednesday — Teacher read:** *Lunch → The Summer Table*
- Lead-in: Think about your own school's lunchroom. How do people usually choose where to sit?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why is choosing where to sit at lunch such a big deal for August? What does "The Summer Table" symbolise?
- Go-away prompt: Describe a time you sat somewhere new (a table, a bus seat, a mat spot) and how it felt.

**Thursday — Group read:** *One to Ten → Jack Will (the chapter)*
- Lead-in: On a scale of 1–10, how "bad" do you think a day can really get before it's actually okay again?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why does August rate his days on a scale of 1 to 10? What does his friendship with Jack Will seem to be turning into?
- Go-away prompt: Rate your best and worst school day ever (1–10) and explain why.

**Weekly Project Menu:**
1. **Poster:** Design an illustrated poster of Mr. Browne's precepts wall — include the "Choose Kind" precept plus 2 more the class invents.
2. **Comic strip:** Show August's 1–10 rating system as a comic, with one panel for a "1" day and one for a "10" day.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Role-play the lunchroom scene — practise showing "unspoken rules" through body language, no dialogue needed.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write about a time you chose to be kind even though it was hard, or a time you wish you had.

**Friday Quiz 2 — 20 Questions**
1. What does August notice as he first enters his classroom? — *People staring/reacting to his appearance.*
2. Who is the English teacher who introduces "precepts"? — *Mr. Browne.*
3. What is a "precept," according to Mr. Browne? — *A rule/motto to live by; a guiding thought.*
4. What is the class's September precept? — *"When given the choice between being right or being kind, choose kind." (accept close paraphrase)*
5. Where does August end up sitting at lunch on his first day? — *Alone, or near "the summer table"/eventually with kids he met over summer.*
6. What is "the summer table"? — *A table of kids August met/knew from a summer program, where he feels safer sitting.*
7. What rating system does August use to describe his days? — *A scale from 1 to 10.*
8. Which student does August start to become real friends with? — *Jack Will.*
9. How does Jack behave differently towards August compared to Julian? — *Jack is friendlier/more genuine, Julian is colder/more awkward.*
10. Inference: Why might August avoid sitting in the middle of the lunchroom? — *Accept reasoned answers: fear of stares, wants to feel less exposed/safe.*
11. Inference: What does the precept "choose kind" suggest about the difference between being right and being kind? — *Accept reasoned answers: sometimes kindness matters more than winning an argument.*
12. Vocabulary: What does "lamb to the slaughter" mean as an expression? — *Someone going innocently into a dangerous or difficult situation.*
13. True or False: August immediately makes lots of friends on his first day. — *False.*
14. Why do you think the author includes Mr. Browne's precepts throughout the whole book? — *Accept reasoned answers: to reinforce the book's kindness theme across every part/narrator.*
15. What does it mean that August "rates" his days like a diary? — *He reflects on and processes how each day felt overall.*
16. Short answer: Why might starting the term with "Choose Kind" as a class rule set the tone for the whole year? — *Open answer.*
17. What small kindness does Jack Will show August that stands out this week? — *Accept reasoned answers, e.g. sitting with him, chatting normally, treating him like any other kid.*
18. Inference: Why might August feel it's safer to "rate" his day than to talk about his feelings directly? — *Accept reasoned answer: easier/safer way to process emotion indirectly.*
19. What is one thing that makes August's first day a "good" moment despite the hard parts? — *Accept reasoned answers: Jack being friendly, a positive interaction.*
20. In your own words, describe the "unwritten rules" of a school lunchroom that August has to learn fast. — *Open answer, mark for insight into social dynamics.*

### WEEK 3 — Halloween & Meeting Via

**Learning Intention:** We can compare how the same events look different depending on whose point of view we're reading from.
**Success Criteria:** I can explain why the author switches narrators to Via, and identify at least one new fact we learn about August from her perspective.

**Monday — Teacher read:** *Wake Me Up when September Ends → Mr. Browne's October Precept*
- Lead-in: What's your favourite thing about Halloween, and why do you think people love dressing up?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why might Halloween be extra complicated for August compared to other kids?
- Go-away prompt: If you designed the "perfect" Halloween costume for yourself, what would it be and why?

**Tuesday — Group read:** *Apples → Halloween*
- Lead-in: Have you ever overheard something you weren't meant to hear? How did it make you feel?
- Comprehension/Inference: What does August overhear on Halloween, and how does it change how he feels about Jack Will? Why is this such a turning point in the story?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a time someone said something (kindly or unkindly) that you weren't meant to hear.

**Wednesday — Teacher read:** *School Pictures → The Bleeding Scream*
- Lead-in: How do you think it feels to see your own photo when you're not happy with how you look?
- Comprehension/Inference: How does August cope after what happened on Halloween? What does "The Bleeding Scream" costume incident reveal about how people react to August when they don't realise he's nearby?
- Go-away prompt: Write a short message of encouragement you'd send August after this tough week.

**Thursday — Group read:** *Names (end Part One) → A Tour of the Galaxy (start Part Two: Via)*
- Lead-in: Whose voice do you think we'll hear from next, now that Part One is over? Why might the author want to tell part of the story through someone else's eyes?
- Comprehension/Inference: What new information do we learn about August's family from Via's opening chapter? How does Via describe her relationship with her brother?
- Go-away prompt: Write 3 things you think Via might find hard about being August's older sister — things August might not even notice.

**Weekly Project Menu:**
1. **Poster:** Design a "Wanted: Kindness" Halloween-themed poster warning against the kind of unkindness Jack's friends showed.
2. **Comic strip:** Illustrate the moment August overhears the conversation on Halloween — show his costume, the setting, and his changing expression.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Perform a short scene from Via's point of view, showing a moment where she has to explain her brother to someone new.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write a diary entry as Via, describing what it's like to be "the sister of the kid everyone stares at."

**Friday Quiz 3 — 20 Questions**
1. What does August wear for Halloween that lets him feel "invisible"? — *A costume/mask (e.g. Bleeding Scream costume) that hides his face.*
2. What does August overhear on Halloween that upsets him deeply? — *Jack Will saying unkind things about him/his appearance to Julian's group, not realising August could hear.*
3. Who is August upset with after Halloween? — *Jack Will.*
4. What happens to August and Jack's friendship after this? — *It breaks down/August stops trusting/talking to Jack.*
5. What is Mr. Browne's October precept about? — *Accept reasoned paraphrase relating to attitude/effort/how you treat others.*
6. Who narrates Part Two of the book? — *Via (August's older sister).*
7. What is the title of Via's opening chapter? — *"A Tour of the Galaxy."*
8. According to Via, how has her family's attention often been focused? — *Mostly on August/his medical needs.*
9. What does Via say she sometimes feels compared to August? — *Overlooked/invisible/less important (accept reasoned answer).*
10. Inference: Why might August choose a costume that completely hides his face for Halloween? — *Accept reasoned answers: wants to feel normal/unnoticed for once.*
11. Inference: What does the timing of the Halloween conversation (August in costume, unrecognised) reveal about how differently Jack acts around August vs. when he thinks August can't hear? — *Accept reasoned answer: possible discomfort/pressure to fit in with the "cool" group.*
12. Vocabulary: What does it mean to feel "invisible," as August does in his costume? — *Unnoticed/unseen, not stared at.*
13. True or False: August enjoys Halloween because it's one of the only days people don't react to his face. — *True.*
14. Why might the author choose to reveal Jack's unkind comment right after a rare "good" day for August? — *Accept reasoned answer: heightens the emotional impact/contrast.*
15. What new perspective on family life do we get once Via starts narrating? — *Accept reasoned answers: her own struggles, jealousy, love for August.*
16. Short answer: Why do you think Via hasn't told her parents how she really feels about always coming second? — *Open answer.*
17. What is the "Punnett Square" she later studies in science hinting at, thematically? — *Genetics/why August looks the way he does (can be flagged as coming up).*
18. Inference: How might August's trust in people change after the Halloween incident? — *Accept reasoned answers: more guarded, less likely to trust new friendships easily.*
19. What does it show about August's character that he still goes trick-or-treating despite what happened? — *Accept reasoned answers: resilience, still wants to be a normal kid.*
20. In your own words, compare August's and Via's biggest worries this week. — *Open answer, mark for comparison quality.*

### WEEK 4 — Via's World & "The Plague"

**Learning Intention:** We can identify cause and effect in a story, and explain how a character's actions affect others around them.
**Success Criteria:** I can explain what "The Plague" refers to and why it affects how other kids treat August's friends.

**Monday — Teacher read:** *Before August → August Through the Peephole*
- Lead-in: Do you think it's possible to love someone and still sometimes feel frustrated by how much attention they get? Why?
- Comprehension/Inference: What do we learn about Via's feelings before August was born? How does the "peephole" image work as a metaphor for how Via sees her family?
- Go-away prompt: Write about someone in your life who takes up "a lot of space" in a good way, and how that makes you feel.

**Tuesday — Group read:** *High School → After School*
- Lead-in: What are you most looking forward to (or nervous about) when you eventually go to high school/intermediate?
- Comprehension/Inference: How is Via's high school experience different from August's at Beecher? Why might Via choose to keep her old friendships from before secret in some ways?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a "fresh start" you'd like to have and what you'd do differently.

**Wednesday — Teacher read:** *The Padawan Bites the Dust → Breakfast*
- Lead-in: What's a nickname you have (or would want) and what does it mean?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why is the "Padawan" nickname significant to August and Jack's friendship? What tension is building at the family breakfast table?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a nickname that means something special to you or a friend.

**Thursday — Group read:** *Genetics 101 → Out with the Old (end Part Two)* + *Weird Kids* (start Part Three: Summer)
- Lead-in: We are about to meet a brand-new narrator called Summer. What do you predict her connection to August might be?
- Comprehension/Inference: What does Via learn in science class that connects to her own family? Why does Summer choose to sit with August — what does this tell us about her character?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a time you chose to sit/stand with someone who was on their own, or a time someone did that for you.

**Weekly Project Menu:**
1. **Poster:** Create a "Meet the Narrators" poster mapping every narrator so far (August, Via, Summer) with one fact and one feeling for each.
2. **Comic strip:** Illustrate Via's "peephole" memory of watching her family focus on August as a baby.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Act out the breakfast table scene, showing the difference between how August and Via each want to talk about their day.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write from Summer's point of view — why she decides to sit with August at lunch on the very first day.

**Friday Quiz 4 — 20 Questions**
1. What nickname does Jack give August, referencing Star Wars? — *"Padawan."*
2. What subject does Via study that links to her family? — *Science/genetics ("Genetics 101" and "The Punnett Square").*
3. What does Via discover or reflect on in her genetics class? — *Accept reasoned answer: the science behind genetic conditions like August's.*
4. What does Via decide about her old best friend Miranda around this time? — *She has drifted apart from Miranda/feels their friendship has changed.*
5. Who is the third narrator introduced in Part Three? — *Summer.*
6. What is the name of Part Three? — *"Summer."*
7. Why does Summer choose to sit with August at the "summer table"? — *Because they share the same first name as the table; she chooses to be kind/genuine, not out of pity.*
8. What does "The Plague" refer to? — *Kids avoiding/not wanting to touch anything August has touched, treating him as if he's contagious.*
9. Who does August believe started "The Plague" rumour? — *Julian (or his group — accept reasoned answer).*
10. How does August feel about "The Plague"? — *Hurt, upset, humiliated.*
11. Inference: Why might Via feel it's easier to start high school where nobody knows about August? — *Accept reasoned answer: wants her own identity, a break from being "August's sister."*
12. Inference: What does the fact that Summer isn't scared to be August's friend suggest about her character compared to other students? — *Accept reasoned answer: genuine, doesn't follow the crowd, empathetic.*
13. Vocabulary: What does "contagious" mean, and why is it an unfair word to use about August? — *Able to spread disease by contact; unfair because his condition isn't contagious at all.*
14. True or False: Summer is friends with August because a teacher told her to be. — *False — she chooses to be his friend genuinely.*
15. Why do you think the author gave Via her own narrated section instead of only telling the story through August? — *Accept reasoned answer: shows the wider impact on the whole family, builds empathy.*
16. Short answer: Explain in your own words what "The Plague" reveals about how rumours spread in a school. — *Open answer.*
17. What does the title "Out with the Old" suggest is happening in Via's life? — *Accept reasoned answer: changing friendships, moving on from her old life/friends.*
18. Inference: Why might August not tell his parents about "The Plague" straight away? — *Accept reasoned answer: doesn't want to worry them, wants to handle it himself, embarrassment.*
19. Compare Jack Will and Summer's kindness towards August — how are they different? — *Open answer: Jack wavers under peer pressure, Summer stays consistently kind.*
20. In your own words, why might real friendship (like Summer's) matter more to August than popularity? — *Open answer.*

### WEEK 5 — Meeting Jack (Part Four)

**Learning Intention:** We can explore how guilt and regret motivate a character's choices.
**Success Criteria:** I can explain why Jack feels guilty and describe what he does to try to make things right.

**Monday — Teacher read:** *The Call → Four Things*
- Lead-in: Have you ever done or said something you instantly regretted? What did you do next?
- Comprehension/Inference: Why does Jack's mum make him call August? What does Jack decide to do differently after Halloween?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a time you had to apologise for something. How did you feel before and after?

**Tuesday — Group read:** *Ex-Friends → Fortune Favors the Bold*
- Lead-in: What would you do if your friends started treating your best friend badly — would you speak up?
- Comprehension/Inference: What happens between Jack and Julian's group now that Jack has changed sides? Why does Jack think "fortune favours the bold"?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a time you had to choose between fitting in and doing what you believed was right.

**Wednesday — Teacher read:** *Private School → Detention*
- Lead-in: What do you think it takes real courage to stand up for someone, even if it costs you something?
- Comprehension/Inference: What happens between Jack and Julian that leads to detention? Why might Jack feel it was worth it?
- Go-away prompt: Write about a moment of real courage you've seen (in real life, a book, or a movie).

**Thursday — Group read:** *Season's Greetings → The War*
- Lead-in: What does it feel like when a whole friend group takes sides against you?
- Comprehension/Inference: How does the "war" between groups of kids affect August and Jack at school? What role does social media/texting play in this part of the story?
- Go-away prompt: Write about the effect that unkind messages (online or on paper) can have, even if "it was just a joke."

**Weekly Project Menu:**
1. **Poster:** Design a "Fortune Favours the Bold" poster celebrating an act of courage/standing up for someone.
2. **Comic strip:** Illustrate the moment Jack defends August against Julian — show the build-up, the action, and the consequence.
3. **Drama/acting scene:** Role-play "The Call" — Jack on the phone trying to apologise to August, showing awkwardness turning into honesty.
4. **Journal reflection:** Write a letter from Jack to August, explaining why he acted the way he did and how he plans to make it right.

**Friday Quiz 5 — 20 Questions**
1. Who narrates Part Four? — *Jack (Jack Will).*
2. Why does Jack's mum make him call August? — *Because she finds out he was unkind about August and wants him to make it right/apologise.*
3. How does Jack feel after Halloween, according to his own narration? — *Guilty, ashamed, regretful.*
4. What does Jack decide to do once school starts again? — *Be a real friend to August again, stand up for him.*
5. Who does Jack get into a physical fight with, defending August? — *Julian.*
6. What punishment does Jack get for the fight? — *Detention (and suspension is also possible — accept reasoned answer).*
7. What does "ex-friends" refer to in this part of the book? — *Jack and Julian's group turning against each other/Jack losing his old friends.*
8. What does Julian's group start doing to Jack once he defends August? — *Excluding him, treating him as an outsider too.*
9. What technology is used to spread unkindness in this part of the story? — *Texts/emails/Facebook messages.*
10. Inference: Why might Jack feel that standing up for August was "worth it" even though he lost friends? — *Accept reasoned answer: values real friendship/doing the right thing over popularity.*
11. Inference: What does Jack's willingness to fight for August suggest has changed in how he sees him? — *Accept reasoned answer: sees him as a true friend, not just an assignment/"the deal."*

## 6. WEEK 10 — FINAL PROJECT (3 lessons) + Presentation & Celebration

No new reading this week — students apply everything they've learned.

### Final Project Options (student choice — pick ONE):
1. **"Choose Kind" Campaign:** Design and pitch a whole-school kindness campaign (posters, a short video script, a pledge wall) inspired by the book's precepts.
2. **Wonder the Musical/Play — Extended Scene:** Write and perform an extended dramatic scene (5+ minutes) either continuing the story or dramatising a moment from a new narrator's point of view (e.g. Julian, told with empathy).
3. **Character Case File:** Create an in-depth multimedia profile (poster + written report + comic panels) tracking one character's journey and change across the whole book, using evidence/quotes (paraphrased, not copied) from every part they appear in.
4. **"Precepts for Our Class" Book:** Design and illustrate an original class book of 12 precepts (one per month of the year), each with an illustration and a real-life example of how to live it out — bound into a class anthology.

### 3-Lesson Structure
- **Lesson 1 (Monday, 45 min):** Choose project, plan (storyboard/outline), gather resources, assign group roles.
- **Lesson 2 (Tuesday, 45 min):** Main production time — writing, drawing, rehearsing, building.
- **Lesson 3 (Wednesday, 45 min):** Finishing touches, rehearsal/polish, prepare for presenting.

### Thursday — Presentation / Gallery Walk Day
Students present or display their final projects. Peer feedback using simple "Glow & Grow" sticky notes (one thing that shone, one thing to consider next time).

### Friday — Celebration & Whole-Unit Reflection ("Choose Kind Day")
- No quiz this week.
- Whole-class reflection circle: share one precept students will carry forward.
- Certificates presented (see image prompts below for a printable certificate design).
- Optional: watch/discuss the book's key themes, share favourite moments from the term.

### Final Project Rubric (NZC Level 3–4 aligned)
| Criteria | Emerging | Achieving | Excelling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Understanding of text (Reading AO: Ideas) | Retells basic plot | Explains character motivations with some evidence | Analyses themes with strong textual evidence |
| Communication of ideas (Writing/Presenting AO) | Ideas are unclear/underdeveloped | Ideas are clear and organised | Ideas are compelling, well-structured, and audience-aware |
| Creativity & effort | Minimal effort/detail | Solid effort, clear care taken | Highly original, exceptional effort and polish |
| Collaboration (if group) | Limited teamwork | Works cooperatively | Leads and supports others effectively |
